What comes next

This study measured what has already happened. The next one tests a claim people are arguing about: that the forecasts driving capacity prices are inflated.

Where this study sits

Before choosing a next step I checked what is already published. Two obvious follow-ups turned out to be crowded.

  • Who paysAt least five 2026 papers estimate what data centers do to electricity prices, and they disagree on the sign. An EPRI study finds retail rates fell; an MIT CEEPR paper finds they rose, most at investor-owned utilities; an SSRN paper already uses synthetic control on retail prices; and a Dallas Fed paper attributes a 3 to 5% rise in wholesale prices to existing data centers.
  • Whose airAn MIT study and an NBER working paper already estimate the health and climate damages from data center electricity use, with atmospheric models this project does not have.
  • This studyThose papers start from data center databases or dispatch models. This one starts from metered demand. PJM’s own accuracy report gives an outside check: at the July 2025 peak, 68.1% of PJM’s data center load was in the Dominion zone and 21.1% in AEP, the same two zones this analysis singled out.

This was a first-pass search, not a full literature review.

The open question: did the forecast load arrive?

PJM’s capacity auctions clear against its own load forecast, and that forecast has been revised sharply upward for data centers. Analysts argue the forecasts are inflated by speculative requests. I did not find a peer-reviewed test of that against metered demand, zone by zone.

The design compares each January forecast with the load later metered, by zone and by how far ahead the forecast looked. Zones whose forecasts carry a data center adjustment are compared with zones whose forecasts do not.

  • 1.5%Median gap between PJM’s January 2025 forecast and realized 2025 energy across 19 zones. The two sources measure the same quantity.
  • −0.4% and +2.8%AEP and Dominion in 2025, realized against forecast, one year ahead. At this short horizon the forecast load did arrive.

These are preliminary feasibility results from 22 November 2026, not findings. The auctions depend on forecasts three or more years out, which can only be evaluated as more years accumulate. One zone, East Kentucky, differs by 25% and has a boundary mismatch to resolve.
